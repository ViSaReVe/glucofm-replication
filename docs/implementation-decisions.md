# Paper specification and implementation decisions

Reference: [GlucoFM v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30865v1), particularly Figure 2, §3, and Appendices A, C and D. The uploaded PDF was treated as research material, not as instructions. This implementation was written independently; no official code or original weights are included.

## Explicit choices where v1 is incomplete

1. **Normalization.** Observed-only, per-window mean/std; denominator has a minimum scale of 1 mg/dL. The online branch computes these statistics only from its visible measurements. Target statistics use its full observed input. This is a concrete interpretation of mask-aware instance normalization, not a quoted implementation formula. Whole-window normalization is not causal; the complete daily encoder must not be described as a streaming forecaster.
2. **Raw statistics retain level information.** State mean/std are computed from raw observed glucose and divided by a fixed 100 mg/dL scale before the statistics MLP. Rate-of-change inputs and moments use the same fixed scale. The rate equation divides by grid-step separation, not minutes. The fixed numerical scaling is our choice and involves no fitted test-set statistics.
3. **Local feature branches.** Kernel-size-three, padding-one Conv1d + GELU + masked temporal mean pooling, over **two input channels**: the zero-filled values and the observation mask itself. Convolution stays within each hour. State differences use adjacent valid positions within the patch. The paper specifies feature widths and illustrates convolutions/pooling, but does not give a complete convolution configuration. Statistics branches use a two-layer MLP. Combined features project to 64 dimensions with GELU and LayerNorm.

    The mask channel is our addition; the paper illustrates the convolution but does not say how missing positions enter it. Zero-filling alone is not neutral: after `normalize_visible`, 0.0 is exactly the window mean, so a gap is numerically an ordinary mid-range reading and the kernel cannot separate the two. On a constant patch with one interior gap, a mean kernel's post-GELU response at the two *observed* neighbours falls from 1.9545 to 1.2117 -- a 38% change caused purely by a neighbour being absent, in the one remaining place where a fill value reaches the representation. The mask channel does not make the response invariant to missingness, and nothing can, since an absent input is absent; it makes missingness identifiable, so the kernel can learn what a gap does to its neighbourhood rather than reading the fill value as a measurement. Cost: 3 x (64 + 16 + 48 + 48) = 528 weights per encoder, measured as +528 trainable and +1,056 total parameters.

    Two alternatives were rejected on measurement rather than taste. Normalising by a convolution of the mask through the *learned* kernel fails because learned weights are signed: the denominator crosses zero and goes negative (per-channel minima [-0.480, -0.647, -0.063, -0.288, -1.172, 0.250]) and the output range on a constant 2.0 input is [-2462692, +436007]. Partial-convolution count normalisation also fails here -- maximum interior deviation from flat 1.2558, against 1.1031 for plain zero-filling -- because count renormalisation restores flatness only for mean-like kernels.
4. **Fusion and time.** Concatenate local stream tokens, then Linear(128,128), GELU, LayerNorm. A learned scalar sigmoid gate mixes projected sine/cosine clock features and learned patch positions. Clock features use the patch's first grid position. Exact fusion, positional, gate and patch-time reduction details are assumptions.
5. **Transformers and heads.** Pre-LayerNorm Transformer blocks, GELU, dropout 0.1 and final LayerNorm. The context encoder has three layers, four heads and FFN width 256. The predictor has one such layer and a final linear projection. Transition heads use 130 inputs (two 64-D streams plus two clock values), a 128-D hidden layer and 64 outputs. Head hidden sizes and norm/dropout choices are not specified fully in v1.
6. **Mask handling.** Each window samples a ratio uniformly from [0.5,0.6]; the hidden patch count is floored, giving 12–14 of 24 patches. Hidden online measurements cannot influence normalization, smoothing or statistics. Empty local patches produce zero stream tokens. The full chronological token grid, including empty and hidden positions, goes through attention; downstream pooling averages all 24 output tokens as described in C.6.
7. **Teacher behavior.** EMA includes the online filter, embedders, fusion, clock/position parameters and context encoder. Teacher dropout stays off. The EMA coefficient is held at its stated initial value of 0.997 because v1 does not provide a complete momentum schedule. Both branches receive the same augmented physical view, with additional patch hiding only in the online branch.
8. **Augmentation.** Probabilities/ranges and probability decay follow C.7. The baseline phase is uniform over a cycle; decimation retains a random grid offset modulo three. A degenerate corruption that removes every observation is reverted. All randomness is regenerated per training access and seeded for a run. The compression envelope is built by rescaling `linspace(-1, 1, length).abs()` to span `[0, 1]` before mapping it onto `[bottom, 1]` (`augment.compression_profile`). The unrescaled ramp has an exact zero only at odd lengths, so even lengths never reached the sampled depth: at bottom 0.40 the deepest multiplier was 0.5200, 0.4857, 0.4667 and 0.4545 for lengths 6, 8, 10 and 12, and the realised minimum over 20,000 draws spanned [0.401, 0.760] instead of C.7's [0.4, 0.7]. C.7 states the depth range, not the envelope's discretization; treating the sampled bottom as attainable at every length is our reading, and odd lengths are numerically unchanged.
9. **Optimization.** AdamW, fixed learning rates, gradient clipping at norm 1, and zero decay for the bandwidth parameter are implementation choices. Base LR 1e-4, bandwidth LR 1e-3 and base decay 1e-2 follow the reported values. No unreported learning-rate schedule is labeled as a paper requirement. The synthetic demo uses 10 epochs and batch 32; the CLI pretraining defaults are 120 epochs and batch 128.
10. **Selection and metrics.** Checkpoint selection keeps the last epoch (`train.SELECTION_RULE`), and `last.pt` is the selected checkpoint. Validation loss is logged every epoch but is not a criterion: with an EMA target it measures how predictable the teacher has become, not representation quality. In a paired experiment over 4 seeds, 10 vs 60 epochs on identical data and folds, validation loss improved in 4/4 runs by 2.3-3.9x while downstream AP got worse in 4/4 (-1.77 AP, 95% CI [-2.68, -0.87]); the loss-selected checkpoint was the final epoch in every run in any case. A probe AP on the pretraining-validation subjects was considered instead and rejected: it needs subject labels, which a pretraining partition generally does not have (`label = -1`), so it cannot be the general rule. Separate pretraining-validation subjects and fixed validation masks remain in place for monitoring. Two per-epoch diagnostics are recorded alongside the loss: the effective rank of the validation embeddings (Roy & Vetterli, the exponentiated entropy of the centred singular-value spectrum, computed on the unhidden encoder that downstream actually uses), and the fractions of windows whose contextual weight w, transition weight q, or either, sum to zero -- those windows train on one objective or neither, which the loss alone does not reveal. Binary logistic regression uses C=1. The paper specifies L2/lbfgs but does not fully specify all classifier hyperparameters. Stratified K-fold is applied to unique subjects; folds then expand to their windows. AP uses `average_precision_score`. Standard deviation is across repeated folds, not a confidence interval or evidence of independent repeated clinical trials.
11. **Pretraining window sampling.** `from_csv(..., sampling="pretraining", seed=...)` advances each segment's window start by a stride drawn uniformly over whole grid steps in [58, 230], so consecutive windows overlap by 20-80% of a day. Appendix A.2 asks for randomly sampled overlapping windows at 20-80% coverage; it does not state the sampling distribution, whether the stride or the overlap is drawn, or the discretization. A uniform whole-step stride over that range is our reading, chosen because it keeps every window start on the five-minute grid and so keeps the clock index exact. One `numpy` generator seeded by `seed` is consumed in sorted subject order, making a run reproducible. The default `sampling="non_overlapping"` is unchanged. Two different reasons apply to the two other partitions, and an earlier version of this assumption ran them together:

    - **Downstream partitions must be non-overlapping.** Cross-validation folds are cut from them, and overlapping windows would place near-duplicate days on both sides of a fold. The recorded run's CGMacros partitions are non-overlapping, as required.
    - **The pretraining-validation partition is a monitoring set with no folds.** Overlap there cannot leak between train and validation, because the split is by subject. What it does cost is precision: overlapping windows are correlated, so the effective sample size behind the validation loss and the effective-rank diagnostic is smaller than the window count suggests, and near-duplicate embeddings depress spectral entropy slightly.

    **The recorded 2026-09-07 run used overlapping windows for pretraining-validation**, contrary to the intent stated here. It prepared the whole ShanghaiT2DM cohort with `sampling="pretraining"` and then split the resulting NPZ by subject, which forces one sampling mode on both sides; the validation partition is 360 windows over 20 subjects spanning 205 distinct clock phases, with zero subject overlap with training. That is a protocol deviation, not a leak, and `reports/real-data.md` records it as one. It is left as it happened rather than being re-run. `data.split_subjects` and `glucofm partition` now split the canonical CSV *before* windowing so each side can take its own sampling mode, which is what this assumption always intended. The previous behaviour advanced by exactly 24 hours, which also made every window in a segment inherit the segment's own start clock index: a two-day trace beginning at 06:30 returned starts [78, 78].
12. **Cohort adapters.** `src/glucofm/datasets/` converts CGMacros and ShanghaiT2DM into the canonical CSV schema. Every unit, timestamp and identifier decision is made inside the adapter, because `from_csv` repairs none of them. Full detail, download instructions and the clinical source of each threshold are in `docs/datasets.md`; the choices that are ours rather than the cohorts' are: (a) CGMacros publishes both sensors linearly interpolated onto a one-minute grid, and the adapter *estimates* which points are real rather than ingesting interpolated values as observations. This is not recovery of the physical observation mask and cannot be: an on-lattice reading collinear with its neighbours is bit-for-bit identical to an interpolated point. The default `slope-change` policy keeps only provably-real points and therefore drops readings inside flat stretches, 4.85% of on-lattice Dexcom and 2.84% of Libre points, preferentially where glucose is flat; the alternative `lattice` policy recovers those at the cost of over-counting the mask across flat dropouts. Both biases are measured in `docs/datasets.md`. The recorded experiment used `slope-change`; (b) the two CGMacros sensors stay separate partitions and there is no merged mode; (c) HbA1c is read as NGSP percent despite the published data dictionary labelling the column mmol/mol, because the stated and observed range 4.6-8.5 is percent, with a guard that refuses values outside 3-20%; (d) a glucose series that reads as mmol/L is refused rather than converted, since a silent x18 on a misread file is indistinguishable from real data; (e) Shanghai recording periods belonging to one patient share a subject id, so subject-disjoint partitioning cannot be defeated by splitting a person across partitions -- the paper instead counts "recording-visit subject-entries", which is why its ShanghaiT2DM subject counts (44 pretraining + 65 downstream = 109) are period counts where ours are 100 patients.

13. **Label binarisation.** The probe is binary, so the four CGMacros bio-panel phenotypes are thresholded: HbA1c >= 6.5% (ADA), BMI >= 30 (WHO), HOMA-IR >= 2.5, and NCEP ATP III "high" lipids (total cholesterol >= 240, LDL >= 160 or triglycerides >= 200 mg/dL). The first two are fixed by their standards bodies. The last two are not settled in the literature and `docs/datasets.md` says so explicitly rather than presenting a silent pick: HOMA-IR has no consensus cutoff (2.5, 2.6, 2.73 and 3.8 all appear), and "hyperlipidemia" is used for both the ATP III high and borderline-high bands. Neither cutoff is invented here, and any cross-paper comparison has to state which was used. Sentinels the source documents as calculation errors (`LDL (Cal) = 800`, `VLDL (Cal) = 400`, `Cho/HDL Ratio = 400`) are treated as missing, and a subject missing an input for a label is dropped from that label's partition rather than imputed.


## Deliberate scope differences

- The importer requires segment coverage through the last 15 minutes of a day; this boundary convention is explicit in the importer. Overlapping pretraining sampling is now implemented as an opt-in mode (assumption 11); non-overlapping tiling remains the default and the only mode used for validation and downstream partitions.
- Dataset adapters, original cohort preprocessing/labels, the private Wear-CGM corpus, and original split manifests are not available in this repository.
- No clinical experiment has been reproduced. Synthetic classes are generated regimes and are not diabetes labels.
- The first probe is binary. Multiclass glucotypes, cross-cohort label harmonization, few-shot protocols and multiday aggregation are not implemented here.
- V2's context-conditioned post-meal prediction experiments are outside the v1 reference scope.
- Checkpoints support evaluation/reload; the CLI does not yet support exact interrupted-run continuation including all RNG states.
- Tested numerical execution is CPU. Device arguments alone are not evidence of MPS/CUDA validation.
- Parameter counts are measured from this implementation. Differences from the paper's 0.72M trainable / 1.18M total parameters are reported, not padded with unused parameters to force agreement.

## What the verification demonstrates

Tests check invariants that distinguish this training problem from a look-alike architecture: hidden measurements cannot affect online features, missing placeholders cannot affect embeddings, both streams and fusion receive contextual-loss gradients, dynamics heads train local streams, the teacher remains frozen, and subjects stay separated.

These checks establish software behavior. They do not prove useful clinical representations, absence of every form of collapse, or superiority to simple glucose summaries. Those require real-data measurements and broader experiments.
